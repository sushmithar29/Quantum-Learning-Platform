Foundation Concept

Wavefunction Collapse

Prior to measurement, a quantum system evolves continuously and unitarily. Measurement breaks unitarity, non-reversibly projecting the state vector onto one of the operator's eigenstates:

P(m) = |\langle m|\psi\rangle|^2   \Rightarrow   |\psi\rangle \xrightarrow{\text{measure}} |m\rangle
🎓 Key Takeaways:
  • Measurement is inherently probabilistic: single outcomes cannot be predicted with certainty.
  • Repeated measurement immediately after collapse deterministically returns the exact same eigenstate.
  • Changing the measurement basis ($Z$ to $X$) alters the eigenstates and observables.

💡 The Measurement Problem
How continuous Schrödinger wave evolution transitions into a discrete macroscopic outcome is known as the Quantum Measurement Problem, giving rise to interpretations like Copenhagen, Many-Worlds, and Decoherence theory.
