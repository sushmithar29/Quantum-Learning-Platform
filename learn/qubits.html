Foundation Concept

What is a Qubit?

A classical bit is strictly deterministic: either 0 or 1. A quantum bit (qubit) is a two-level quantum physical system represented by a unit vector in a 2D complex Hilbert space $\mathbb{C}^2$:

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩   (where |α|² + |β|² = 1)
🎓 Key Takeaways:
  • Basis states $|0\rangle = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $|1\rangle = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ are orthogonal eigenvectors.
  • Amplitudes $\alpha$ and $\beta$ can be complex numbers, encoding relative phase.
  • Measuring the qubit yields outcome 0 with probability $|\alpha|^2$ and 1 with probability $|\beta|^2$.

💡 Physical Implementations
In physical hardware, qubits can be realized as superconducting transmon circuits (Josephson junctions), trapped ions (hyperfine ground/excited states), photonic polarization modes, or silicon quantum dots.
Born Rule: Total measurement probability is strictly conserved: P(|0⟩) + P(|1⟩) = 1.00 (100%).
