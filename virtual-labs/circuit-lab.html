🎯 Section 01

Experiment Aim

To design, synthesize, and simulate quantum logic circuits on multi-qubit registers, verify the unitary action of single-qubit quantum gates (H, X, Y, Z, S, T) and entangling multi-qubit gates (CNOT, SWAP), create maximally entangled Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (Bell) states, and analyze the resulting state vector amplitudes, density matrix components, and projective measurement distributions under statistical sampling.

📖 Section 02

Theoretical Foundations

1. The Qubit and Complex Hilbert Space

In quantum information, a quantum bit (qubit) is a two-level physical system described as a unit vector in a 2-dimensional complex Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_2$. The orthonormal computational basis states are denoted in Dirac notation as:

|0⟩ = [1, 0]ᵀ, |1⟩ = [0, 1]ᵀ
|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩, where α, β ∈ ℂ and |α|² + |β|² = 1

2. Single-Qubit Unitary Operators

Any closed time-evolution of a quantum state is governed by a unitary transformation $U$ satisfying $U^\dagger U = I$. Key elementary quantum gates include:

• Hadamard (H): H = (1/√2) [ [1, 1], [1, -1] ] → H|0⟩ = |+⟩, H|1⟩ = |-⟩
• Pauli-X (NOT): X = [ [0, 1], [1, 0] ] → X|0⟩ = |1⟩, X|1⟩ = |0⟩
• Pauli-Y: Y = [ [0, -i], [i, 0] ]
• Pauli-Z: Z = [ [1, 0], [0, -1] ] → Z|1⟩ = -|1⟩
• Phase Gate S: S = [ [1, 0], [0, i] ] → S = √Z
• T Gate (π/8): T = [ [1, 0], [0, e^(iπ/4)] ] → T² = S

3. Multi-Qubit Entanglement & Controlled-NOT (CNOT)

Composite quantum systems reside in the tensor product space $\mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B$. The Controlled-NOT gate acts on two qubits, flipping the target qubit conditioned on the control qubit:

CNOT = |0⟩⟨0| ⊗ I + |1⟩⟨1| ⊗ X

Applying H on q₀ and CNOT(q₀ → q₁) on initial state |00⟩:
|00⟩ ──[ H ⊗ I ]──> (|00⟩ + |10⟩)/√2 ──[ CNOT ]──> |Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2

The state $|\Phi^+\rangle$ is a maximally entangled Bell state that cannot be factored into independent single-qubit states $|\psi_A\rangle \otimes |\psi_B\rangle$, demonstrating non-local quantum correlations.

Learning Objectives

  • Construct single-qubit superpositions using the Hadamard gate and verify probability amplitude equality.
  • Synthesize two-qubit entangled states (Bell states) using a cascade of Hadamard and CNOT gates.

📋 Section 04

Step-by-Step Procedure

01

Initialize Quantum Register

Navigate to the Simulation tab. Choose the number of qubits (2 or 3) from the control panel. By default, all qubits are prepared in ground state |00⟩.

02

Place Superposition Gates

Click on the [H] (Hadamard) gate from the Gate Palette, then click the first slot on wire q0. This transforms qubit 0 into superposition (|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2.

03

Create Entanglement with CNOT

Select the [CNOT] gate and click on column 3 of the circuit. A control dot (●) appears on q0 and a target target (⊕) appears on q1, entangling both wires.

04

Set Simulation Parameters

Adjust the Shots slider to 512 or 1024 to establish high statistical confidence. Set the noise slider to 0% for ideal unitary evolution.

05

Execute & Analyze Results

Click "Run Simulation". Watch the animated quantum pulse travel across the wires, observe gate flashes, and verify the resulting 50:50 distribution of |00⟩ and |11⟩.

👁️ Section 06

Observation Analysis

During execution of the quantum circuit, multiple qualitative and quantitative phenomena can be systematically recorded:

A

Superposition Induction

When the Hadamard gate acts on |0⟩, probability distributes uniformly between |0⟩ and |1⟩ with equal amplitudes 1/√2 ≈ 0.707. No preferred computational basis exists.

B

Entanglement Correlation

For the Bell state (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2, the individual qubits possess no definite state on their own, yet measurement results are strictly correlated: |01⟩ and |10⟩ exhibit 0% occurrence in an ideal system.

C

Decoherence Degradation

As simulated environmental noise increases above 0%, spurious counts appear in forbidden states (|01⟩, |10⟩), causing state purity Tr(ρ²) to drop below unity.

✍️ Section 09

Experimental Assignments

01

Synthesize the 3-Qubit GHZ State

Construct a 3-qubit circuit that produces |GHZ⟩ = (|000⟩ + |111⟩)/√2. Verify using the simulator that no other basis states appear in the outcome histogram.

02

Analyze Quantum State Swap Without SWAP Gate

Implement a SWAP operation using exactly three consecutive CNOT gates alternating control and target wires: CNOT(q0→q1), CNOT(q1→q0), CNOT(q0→q1). Prove that the states of q0 and q1 are completely interchanged.
