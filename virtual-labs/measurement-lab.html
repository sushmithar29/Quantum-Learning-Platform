🎯 Section 01

Experiment Aim

To investigate the quantum measurement postulate by preparing arbitrary single-qubit states, choosing projective measurement bases (Z-basis, X-basis, Y-basis), performing repeated shot measurements to accumulate statistical distributions, and verifying that empirical outcome frequencies converge to the theoretical Born-rule probabilities |⟨m|ψ⟩|² as the number of shots increases.

📖 Section 02

Theoretical Foundations

1. The Quantum Measurement Postulate

Quantum measurement is the interface between the quantum system and the classical observer. Every observable A corresponds to a Hermitian operator. For a measurement of observable M with spectral decomposition M = Σ mₖ Pₖ where Pₖ are orthogonal projectors, acting on state |ψ⟩:

Born Rule: P(outcome m) = ⟨ψ|Pₘ|ψ⟩ = |⟨m|ψ⟩|²

Post-measurement state: |ψ'⟩ = Pₘ|ψ⟩ / √P(m) (state collapse)

2. Measurement Bases

The Z-basis {|0⟩, |1⟩} is the computational basis. To measure in the X-basis {|+⟩, |-⟩} or Y-basis {|i⟩, |-i⟩}, a basis rotation must be applied before the standard Z measurement:

Z-basis: measure directly (no pre-rotation)
X-basis: apply H† before measurement (H|+⟩=|0⟩, H|-⟩=|1⟩)
Y-basis: apply S†·H† before measurement (transforms Y eigenstates to Z)

For state |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ measured in Z-basis:
P(0) = |α|², P(1) = |β|²

3. Statistical Convergence (Law of Large Numbers)

Each measurement is an independent Bernoulli trial. After N shots, empirical frequencies fₙ = nₘ/N converge to P(m) by the law of large numbers. The statistical error scales as:

σ(f) = √(P(1-P)/N) → 0 as N → ∞
🎓 Section 03

Learning Objectives

  • Apply Born's rule to compute theoretical measurement probabilities from complex amplitudes α and β.
  • Distinguish between projective measurement in the Z-basis, X-basis, and Y-basis using appropriate pre-rotations.
  • Observe irreversible wavefunction collapse: after measuring |1⟩, the state is no longer in superposition.
  • Quantify statistical convergence: track how empirical shot frequencies approach theoretical probabilities as shot count N increases.
  • Interpret histograms of repeated quantum measurements and relate them to probability distributions.

Step-by-Step Procedure

01

Prepare Quantum State

Use the amplitude sliders to set the real amplitude |α| of the state |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + e^(iφ)β|1⟩. The normalization |α|²+|β|²=1 is enforced automatically.

02

Choose Measurement Basis

Select the measurement basis: Z-basis (default), X-basis (applies H before readout), or Y-basis (applies S†H before readout). Observe how the theoretical probabilities change.

03

Set Shot Count

Adjust the shots slider from 1 to 1024. For small N, observe high variance; for large N (≥ 512), the empirical histogram converges to Born-rule theoretical values.

04

Run Measurement

Click "Run Measurement" to execute all shots. The histogram animates, showing the accumulated statistics bar by bar, with a theoretical dotted reference line shown in cyan.

05

Analyze & Repeat

Compare the empirical frequency (green bar) with the theoretical probability (cyan dashed line). Change the shot count and re-run to observe statistical variance narrowing.

👁️ Section 06

Observation Analysis

A

State Collapse Irreversibility

After measuring a superposition state (|+⟩) in the Z-basis and obtaining result 0, the state collapses to |0⟩. A second Z-measurement now always gives 0 — the coherence is permanently destroyed.

B

Basis-Dependent Statistics

The state |+⟩ gives P(0)=P(1)=50% in the Z-basis, but P(+)=100%, P(-)=0% in the X-basis. The same quantum state gives deterministic outcomes in one basis but random in another.

C

Statistical Convergence

With N=10 shots, empirical frequencies may deviate significantly from theory (σ≈16%). At N=1024, the histogram closely matches theory with σ≈1.5%, visually demonstrating the law of large numbers.
